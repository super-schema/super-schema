# Trademark policy

The Super Schema **code** is licensed under the [Apache License 2.0](LICENSE). The license
covers the code only. Section 6 of the license states that it grants no rights to the
project's names or logos.

This policy covers the name **"Super Schema"** and the Super Schema logo (`src/app/icon.svg`
and the artwork in `docs/brand/`), together called "the marks". The marks identify the
official project created by Jay Sen. The goal is simple: people should always be able to
tell the official project apart from forks and products built on it.

## You may, without asking

- **Describe the origin** of your work factually, for example "based on Super Schema",
  "a fork of Super Schema", "built with Super Schema", or "compatible with Super Schema".
- **Refer to the project** by name in articles, talks, documentation, comparisons, and
  tutorials, and link to it.
- **Redistribute the unmodified official project** under its own name, with the
  `LICENSE` and `NOTICE` files kept intact.

## Please don't, without written permission

- Name a fork, product, service, company, or domain "Super Schema", or anything confusingly
  similar (for example "SuperSchema Cloud" or "Super-Schema Pro").
- Use the logo, or a modified version of it, for a modified version or a derived product.
- Suggest that your product or service is official, endorsed, certified, or supported by the
  Super Schema project or its author when it isn't.

If you modify Super Schema and distribute it or offer it as a service, give it a **different
name** and credit the original, for example: "Acme Schemas, based on
[Super Schema](https://github.com/super-schema/super-schema) by Jay Sen".

## Credit

The Apache License requires anyone who distributes Super Schema, or a work derived from it,
to keep the attribution in the [`NOTICE`](NOTICE) file. Beyond that requirement, if you build
on Super Schema we ask that you credit the project and its author, Jay Sen, somewhere your
users can see it, such as your README, About page, or documentation.
